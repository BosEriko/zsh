# Anti-AI Frontend Design

## Goal

Create websites that feel intentionally designed by a skilled human designer, rather than assembled from common AI-generated templates.

## Design Principles

1. **Distinct visual identity**
   - Establish a clear design direction before implementation.
   - Choose typography, colors, spacing, and layout appropriate to the product.
   - Avoid defaulting to generic SaaS aesthetics.

2. **Intentional layouts**
   - Avoid repetitive three-column card grids.
   - Use varied compositions when appropriate.
   - Establish strong visual hierarchy.
   - Prefer meaningful whitespace over excessive decoration.

3. **Typography**
   - Select fonts based on the brand's personality.
   - Use deliberate type scales and line heights.
   - Avoid relying exclusively on generic font combinations.
   - Make typography a defining part of the design.

4. **Color**
   - Use a restrained, coherent palette.
   - Avoid default purple/blue gradients.
   - Use gradients only when they serve the visual direction.
   - Maintain accessible contrast.

5. **Components**
   - Avoid unnecessary cards, borders, and shadows.
   - Don't wrap every piece of content in a container.
   - Use consistent components without making every section identical.
   - Prefer purposeful details over decoration.

6. **Content**
   - Avoid generic AI marketing language.
   - Use specific, product-relevant headings.
   - Prefer authentic product screenshots and meaningful visuals.
   - Never invent testimonials, metrics, or customer logos.

## Review Checklist

Before completing frontend work:

- Does the interface resemble a generic AI-generated SaaS template?
- Are there unnecessary gradients, shadows, or rounded cards?
- Is the typography distinctive and appropriate?
- Does the layout have a clear visual hierarchy?
- Is the design consistent without being repetitive?
- Does the website communicate a recognizable identity?
- Does the design work on mobile and desktop?
- Is the interface accessible and usable?

## Execution

1. Inspect the existing design system and brand guidelines.
2. Identify repetitive or generic design patterns.
3. Propose a clear visual direction.
4. Implement improvements while preserving functionality.
5. Review desktop and mobile layouts.
6. Remove unnecessary visual decoration.
7. Check accessibility and consistency.
8. Explain the design decisions.

## Important

Do not blindly remove gradients, cards, shadows, or rounded corners. These are valid design tools. Remove them only when they are unnecessary, repetitive, or inconsistent with the intended visual identity.

The goal is not to make websites look unusual. The goal is to make them look deliberate, memorable, and appropriate for their audience.
